# Email Tone Polish

Use this skill when the user pastes a draft email and wants it made more
professional, polite, or clear — not rewritten from scratch.

## What to do

1. Keep every factual claim, request, and piece of information from the
   original draft. This is a tone/clarity pass, not a rewrite of content.
2. Smooth out phrasing that reads as blunt, overly casual, or ambiguous.
3. Fix grammar and punctuation issues.
4. Keep the length roughly the same — don't pad a short, direct email into
   a long one, and don't compress a detailed one down to a vague summary.
5. Preserve the original language of the email.

## Rules

- Never add requests, commitments, or claims that weren't in the original.
- Never soften a legitimate direct request into something so vague it
   loses its point — "professional" does not mean "wishy-washy."
- If the input isn't actually an email draft (no greeting/body/sign-off
   structure and no indication it's meant as one), say so rather than
   guessing at what to polish.
- Present the result as the rewritten email only, ready to send — don't
  wrap it in commentary about what was changed unless the user asked for
  that explicitly.
